3. KEY FINDING — C2 COUNT IS ANTI-CORRELATED WITH MALICE
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    corr(P(malware), num_c2) = -0.158
    median num_c2: benign (F-Droid) 5  >  malware 3
    false negatives (n=17): median num_c2 12; 11/17 have >=5 C2 domains
    (ANATOMY NOTE: computed on the pre-c2_alignment bundle — current
     bundle: FP 0, FN 10, 15 features; see §2.)

    The 11 high-C2 FN are the PACKED-DROPPER population: every one has
    num_payloads >=1 AND num_encodings >=1 (counts frequently saturated at
    500), 8/11 with native libs, all labelled to generic/unknown GT families
    (AndroidOS, Agent, Gen, unknown). Their observable surface (domains,
    string counts, permissions) is indistinguishable from benign SDK apps
    (Firebase/Crashlytics/AdMob embed many domains). The malicious payload
    stays inside encoded blobs that the 20-feature vector does not see.

    Takeaway: raw C2 quantity is a weak/anti-predictive signal in this corpus.
    Domain ALIGNMENT with known family profiles (c2_alignment in the fusion,
    weight 0.25) is the discriminator. Counts are not. Structure features
    (classes, strings, permissions) carry the signal. Empirical confirmation:
    removing c2_alignment from the binary bundle costs -0.79pts CV
    (0.9057 -> 0.8979) — alignment is load-bearing where counts are noise.

    Observation (flag for Reet, not claimed): on every FN row
    num_payloads == num_encodings exactly — check
    analysis/gap_features_cache.json to confirm whether payload and encoding
    counters double-count the same blobs.

6. DRAFT THESIS SECTIONS (corrected numbers)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Family — honest framing:
"Our family confidence fusion improves ranking accuracy from 60.4% to 68.2%
on the 192 scorable samples — but the gain concentrates on high-quality GT
sources: androzoo_drebin improves to 86.2% while abusech (78% catch-all
labels, no static profiles) correctly abstains. On the samples the system
answers with confidence, precision is 100%. Predicting 'unknown' is correct
when the ground truth label is itself catch-all."

Binary:
"Our Random Forest achieves 90.6% accuracy under 5-fold cross-validation
(seed 0; robust across 5/5 seeds, 90.56-92.93). The 98.04% in-sample figure
reflects only per-prediction explanation use; in-sample optimism is -7.47pts
of modest overfitting. The deployed feature set is 15 features: six raw C2
count/chain features are dropped, and c2_alignment — a domain-match score
against known-family profiles — is the only C2-derived input. Ablation
confirms the permission/structure features carry the signal: removing
c2_alignment costs 0.79pts, while the raw C2 counts are not in the model at
all. Coverage caveat: c2_alignment is observed for 199/509 cache rows (all
malware; no-cache and empty-domain rows score 0.0), benign values are
structurally 0.0 by assumption, and deployment recomputes the value fresh
from every APK."

C2 finding (publishable):
"Counterintuitively, benign samples in our dataset contain median 5 plaintext
domains vs. malware median 3 (corr = -0.158). This reflects SDK embedding:
Firebase, Crashlytics and AdMob hardcode domains. Packed malware, conversely,
contains encrypted/junk string blobs that parse as domains. Raw C2 count is
therefore anti-correlated with malice. Domain alignment with known malware
families (family_confidence_scorer) is the discriminator; raw counts are not.
This informs our ensemble design: C2 contributes via profile match (0.25
weight, §5.2), not presence."
